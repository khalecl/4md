## Why We Are Auditing

### Energy is our largest controllable cost
- **32%**: of operating spend goes on electricity
- **61%**: of that electricity is used for cooling
- **18%**: typical saving found by a first audit
<!-- notes: These figures come from last year's utility bills across the six sites. Cooling dominates because of the climate, so most of the opportunity is in the chilled-water plant. -->

### Four reasons to act now
- **Cost**: tariffs rose twice in the last two years
- **Compliance**: ISO 50001 certification is due next year
- **Carbon**: Scope 2 cuts are needed for the ESG report
- **Reliability**: old chillers are failing more often
<!-- notes: Each of these alone would justify the audit; together they make it urgent. -->

### The key insight
Most savings come from controls, not new equipment.
<!-- notes: This is the single most important message. Expensive retrofits come later; schedules, setpoints and sequencing come first and cost little. -->

## How The Audit Works

### Five steps over twelve weeks
1. **Scope**: agree sites and data access
2. **Measure**: install loggers on main loads
3. **Survey**: walk-through of every plant room
4. **Analyse**: baseline and savings model
5. **Report**: costed measures and M&V plan
<!-- notes: Steps two and three overlap. The loggers need at least two weeks of data before analysis starts. -->

### What we will look at
- HVAC
    - Chiller sequencing
    - AHU schedules
    - Setpoint resets
- Lighting
    - LED retrofit
    - Occupancy sensors
- Metering
    - Sub-meters per floor
    - BMS trending

> The cheapest kilowatt-hour is the one you never use. — Energy management maxim

## Key Takeaways
- **Controls first**: the cheapest savings come before any capital spend
- **Meter everything**: no data, no decisions
- **People matter**: staff behaviour drives a fifth of use
- **Verify**: report savings with an agreed M&V method
